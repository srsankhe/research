STUDY OVERVIEW
===================================================================================

Study Title: Flow cytometric markers identify tubular epithelial cells in urine
             as indicators of kidney tubular damage

Research Goal: Identify and validate cell markers for detecting kidney tubular
               damage in urine samples

Total Study Size: 243 patients

===================================================================================
STUDY DESIGN - TWO PHASES
===================================================================================

PHASE 1: DISCOVERY (Single-cell sequencing)
-------------------------------------------
Sample Size: 8 urine samples
Patients: AKI and glomerular disease
Method: Single-cell RNA-seq + CITE-seq (Cellular Indexing of Transcriptomes
        and Epitopes by Sequencing)
Data Available: YES - This is what's in the GEO database (GSE282344)
Purpose: Identify cell markers that indicate kidney tubular damage

Samples Analyzed:
  - GSM8641663_NR_JK_047 - 33,538 genes × 2,772 cells (control)
  - GSM8641664_NR_JK_048 - 33,538 genes × 2,741 cells (control)
  - GSM8641665_NR_JK_049 - 33,538 genes × 3,136 cells (treatment)
  - GSM8641666_NR_JK_053 - 33,538 genes × 1,914 cells (treatment)

Samples Excluded from DESeq2:
  - GSM8641667_NR_JK_050 - Protein markers only (6 CD markers)
  - GSM8641668_NR_JK_051 - Protein markers only (6 CD markers)
  - GSM8641669_NR_JK_052 - Protein markers only (6 CD markers)
  - GSM8641670_NR_JK_054 - Protein markers only (6 CD markers)


PHASE 2: VALIDATION (Flow cytometry)
-------------------------------------
Sample Size: 235 patients across 4 cohorts
  - AKI patients (n=63)
  - COVID-19 infection (n=47)
  - ANCA-associated vasculitis (n=110)
  - Healthy controls (n=15)
Method: Flow cytometry
Data Available: NO - Not in GEO database
Purpose: Validate markers found in discovery phase using clinical method

===================================================================================
WHY ONLY 8 SEQUENCING SAMPLES?
===================================================================================

Single-cell RNA sequencing:
  - Expensive (~$1,000-3,000 per sample)
  - Computationally intensive
  - Discovery-focused - identifies what markers to look for

Flow cytometry:
  - Cheaper (~$50-200 per sample)
  - Faster
  - Validation-focused - confirms markers work in clinical settings

Strategy: Use expensive sequencing on small sample to discover markers,
          then validate with cheaper method on large cohort

===================================================================================
PROTEIN MARKERS TESTED (CITE-seq data)
===================================================================================

Six CD markers were tested using antibody capture:
  1. CD10 (TotalSeq-B0062 anti-human CD10) - MME/Neprilysin
  2. CD13 (TotalSeq-B0364 anti-human CD13)
  3. CD24 (TotalSeq-B0180 anti-human CD24)
  4. CD44 (TotalSeq-B0073 anti-human CD44)
  5. CD133 (TotalSeq-B0952 Streptavidin binding anti-human CD133)
  6. CD326 (TotalSeq-B0123 anti-human CD326)

These markers were specifically chosen to identify tubular epithelial cells
(TECs) in urine.

===================================================================================
MME (CD10) - DETAILED FINDINGS
===================================================================================

Gene Name: MME (Membrane Metalloendopeptidase)
Also Known As:
  - CD10 (cluster of differentiation 10)
  - Neprilysin
  - CALLA (Common Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia Antigen)

STATISTICAL RESULTS FROM DESeq2:
---------------------------------
baseMean:           1,487.4 (moderately high expression)
log2FoldChange:     1.04 (treatment vs control)
                    → ~2.06-fold upregulation in treatment (2^1.04)
Standard Error:     0.93
Test Statistic:     1.12
p-value:            0.26
Adjusted p-value:   0.93

SIGNIFICANCE: NOT STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT
  - Did NOT make it into significant genes list
  - Adjusted p-value (0.93) >> significance threshold (0.05)
  - Shows trend toward upregulation but not statistically reliable

INTERPRETATION:
---------------------------------
The slight upward trend (1.04 log2FC) without statistical significance suggests:
  - MME expression is variable across samples
  - Effect size is small and inconsistent
  - NOT a key differentially expressed gene in treatment vs control
  - However, CD10 protein levels (measured by CITE-seq) may show different
    patterns than mRNA levels

BIOLOGICAL FUNCTION:
---------------------------------
  - Cell surface enzyme that cleaves peptides
  - Regulates peptide signaling
  - Important in immune cell development and function
  - Marker for various cell types:
    * Germinal center B cells
    * Certain leukemias/lymphomas
    * Stem/progenitor cells
    * Tubular epithelial cells (in kidney context)

CLINICAL RELEVANCE:
---------------------------------
In the context of this kidney damage study, CD10/MME was likely chosen because:
  - Expressed on tubular epithelial cells
  - Can help identify damaged kidney cells shed into urine
  - Useful flow cytometry marker for clinical validation

IMPORTANT CAVEATS
===================================================================================

⚠️ SAMPLE CONDITION ASSIGNMENTS ASSUMED:
  - Samples 047, 048 = control
  - Samples 049, 053 = treatment

  These assignments were inferred and should be verified against:
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE282344

⚠️ PSEUDO-BULK ANALYSIS:
  - Single-cell data was aggregated to pseudo-bulk for DESeq2
  - This loses cell-type-specific information
  - Consider single-cell-specific analysis methods for detailed insights

⚠️ PROTEIN VS mRNA:
  - RNA expression (what DESeq2 measures) doesn't always correlate with
    protein levels (what CITE-seq/flow cytometry measures)
  - CD markers may show different patterns at protein level
